# Literature review — palette extraction state of the art

Produced 2026-07-31 by the literature-scout agent (report persisted by the orchestrator; the
agent's sandbox blocked report writes). Full findings verbatim below; the TOP-8 section is the
actionable summary, each idea mapped to our open problem classes P1–P6:

P1 identity-direction diversity · P2 segmentation granularity (texture-vs-structure,
pointillist lettering) · P3 text-as-foreground semantics · P4 non-linear gradient/midpoint
modeling · P5 surface-role selection · P6 generalization measurement.

## TOP 8, COMPRESSED

1. **I3 — Stroke Width Transform as orthogonal mark evidence (P3, P2; M).** Epshtein CVPR2010. Rays from edge pixels to antiparallel-gradient edges; median-SWT per ray; CC filter `std/mean < 0.5`, `diameter/medianSWT < 10`; chains of ≥3 collinear components = text line. **Speckle has no consistent stroke width** — the std/mean gate annihilates it while lettering passes by construction. This is shape evidence orthogonal to color, which is exactly the `knuckles` failure (lettering shares a family with noise). Our `repetition` term is a weak proxy for what SWT measures directly. Feed `strokeWidthConsistency` + `collinearChainLength` into the existing `markSupportOf` product gate (the `strokeCoverage` slot already exists), then reconsider the deliberate exclusion of mark evidence from obligation nomination — that's the direct route to `krafty`. Run at FULL RES (charter forbids downsampling; thin strokes are what downsampling destroys); expect a few hundred ms. libccv production constants: `std_ratio 0.83`, `thickness_ratio 1.5`, `height_ratio 1.7`, `distance_ratio 2.9`, `letter_thresh 3`.

2. **I12/I11/I13 — split candidate RECALL from RANKING (P6; S; highest value/hour).** Warehouse measured: 72 records / 48 images, but only **1** per-role correction recorded. Ask reviewers to pick all 4 role colors from the swatch chips on *every* item (the chip mechanism already reproduces Lin & Hanrahan's candidate-generation step: exact pixels, ≥0.02%/48px support). Then compute two separate numbers: does our ≤1500-candidate domain *contain* a treatment within ε of the human answer (recall), and how far down did we rank it. `vvbrown` ("Pareto-dominated and unreachable"), `loups` ("zero gradient hypotheses"), `elephunk` ("omitted entirely") were all **recall** failures debugged as ranking failures. Score with min-cost bipartite matching in OKLab; report three lines — human–human ceiling, oracle-over-candidates, us. Lin & Hanrahan found humans agree on only ~2 of 5 swatches, so our ceiling is well below 100%.

3. **I2 — texture/noise admissibility gate for surface promotion (P2, P5; S).** Immerkær's 3×3 mask `[[1,-2,1],[-2,4,-2],[1,-2,1]]`, `σ ≈ sqrt(π/2)·Σ|I∗N|/(6(W-2)(H-2))`. It's a difference of two Laplacians so it annihilates locally *linear* intensity — **a smooth gradient field returns σ≈0 while grain returns large σ**, which separates "textured field" from "gradient field" without confusing them. Gate: a candidate surface whose mean ΔE from its parent field is within ~2–3σ of the parent's measured noise is grain, not a surface. Pair with **area opening** (Vincent 1993: removes components < λ *without deforming survivors*, union-find O(Nα(N)) — we already have areas) and the **h-maxima transform** (contrast analogue; grain is low-area AND low-h simultaneously). This is a hard gate on role promotion, not another soft term — our `textureCalm` at weight 0.15 can be outvoted, which is `meteora`.

4. **I8 — adopt Lin & Hanrahan's human-fitted scoring terms (P1, P5; M).** CHI 2013, 1600 human themes over 40 images (incl. a Pointillist stratum), LASSO over 79 features. Their model reaches human parity: 20.4 CIELAB vs artists' 20.6; Likert 3.54 vs k-means 3.14. Key weights: **soft segment recoloring error weighted by salient density −9.97**; **between-variance −2.14**; **mean pairwise CIELAB diversity +1.56**; hard *uniform* recoloring error **+0.94 (opposite sign)**; **saturation-range coverage +0.87**; segment uniqueness +0.71. Two implications: (a) our `fieldScore` is essentially a uniform-coverage objective, and the literature says that flips sign in importance once segment weighting exists; (b) the +1.56/−2.14 pair is a human-fitted statement of P1 — spread out, don't clump — which we currently express as *hard thresholds* (`materialDistance 0.025`, hue gates) rather than a continuous term. Segment uniqueness (negative entropy of `p(color|segment)`) formalizes both the `skap` principle already in our code and the `meteora` "surface restates the background" rejection.

5. **I6 + I5 — hue-direction support prior, then continuous diversity (P1; S then S/M).** MCU's "excited proportion" sums population over a **±15° hue neighbourhood**, i.e. it measures support for a *hue direction*, not a *family*. Our obligation lane slots are families, so several populous near-neutral families each claim a slot independently — literally P1. A 360-bin chroma-weighted hue histogram with ±15° smoothing is ~10 lines, O(N), deterministic, and near-neutrals can't inflate it. Then replace the hard chroma/hue gates in identity-direction selection with **greedy log-det / DPP MAP** over `q_i·v_i` (evidence-level quality × unit chroma direction): `det(L_Y)` is the squared volume spanned by the selected set, greedy MAP is deterministic and near-optimal, and it degrades gracefully on many-colored artwork instead of thinning to whatever survives a hue cliff. Gate by source support *before* the log-det step or it will pick vivid noise (that's `slim`).

6. **I7 — multi-stop gradients via spatial-`t` regression + Douglas–Peucker (P4; M).** Fit `t = a·x+b·y+c` (linear) or `t = |p−c|` (radial, solving for the centre) at native resolution over the field mask. Then run DP on the `t`-sorted binned OKLab color path at ε ≈ 1 JND (0.02). **DP's retained vertices are input points** — stops are drawn from the observed sequence and snapping to a real supported pixel is a nearest-neighbour lookup, not synthesis. Accept on **p95** per-pixel ΔE residual, not mean (mean hides a bad midpoint — the `doja`/`placebo` endpoint-fidelity complaint), and select among {solid, linear, radial, multi-stop} by `residual + λ·(#stops)`. **That MDL penalty is a mechanism for gradient neutrality**: an unjustified gradient costs something, symmetrically with an unjustified flat. Isotonic regression (PAVA, O(n)) is the right variant under monotone lightness — its level-set boundaries are natural stops and it formalizes the existing `monotonicity` term. B-splines are strictly worse for us (control points aren't image colors).

7. **I1 — Felzenszwalb–Huttenlocher predicate as our RAG merge rule (P2, P5; S/M).** Merge iff `Diff(C1,C2) ≤ min(Int(C1)+k/|C1|, Int(C2)+k/|C2|)`. The `k/|C|` term makes small regions require proportionally stronger evidence (a 30px speckle needs `Diff > Int + k/30`; grain never achieves it), and `Int(C)` is **adaptive** — an internally noisy region accumulates high internal difference so further grain edges get absorbed. That is the textbook formalization of "texture belongs to one field," and it's the segmentation Lin & Hanrahan used for their highest-weight features. Apply over our *existing components* (nodes = components, weight = OKLab ΔE), replacing the flat `FIELD_COMPOSITE_EMBEDDED_BOUNDARY_FRACTION = 0.75` with a size-adaptive rule; O(E log E), milliseconds. **Determinism hinges entirely on a stable edge sort** — with quantized families, ties are common, so break by `(componentId, neighbourId)`. Highest regression risk in the set: do it after the recall/ranking metric exists.

8. **I4 — convex hull as a DIRECTION GENERATOR only (P1; M/L; highest risk).** Tan/Lien/Gingold TOG2016: progressive-hull simplification, each edge contraction must strictly add volume so the hull always still contains every image color; objective = minimize added volume; deterministic, seconds (RGBXY 2018 does 6MP in 20ms). Their claim is exactly our P1: hull vertices find *"simpler and even hidden colors"* — the saturated generating colors clustering averages away. Our greedy nearest-anchor ball has the same failure mode: a vivid sparse direction gets swallowed by a populous near-neutral. **Hard conflict: hull vertices are not real image colors and can be imaginary** (Wang CGF2019 makes exactly this criticism). Mapping: compute the hull in OKLab over *populated* bins with a support floor, use each vertex only to assert "this chromatic direction exists," then snap through existing `sourceSupport()`. Risk: chroma noise and JPEG aberration live on the hull surface by definition.

## FULL REPORT

### 0. Calibration fact used throughout
OKLab Euclidean JND ≈ **0.02** (OKLab L is 0–1 vs CIELAB 0–100, so ΔE_ok ≈ ΔE_ab/100; ΔE00 ≈ 1 ⇒ 0.02). Therefore `FAMILY_BIN_STEP 0.04` ≈ 2 JND, `FAMILY_ANCHOR_RADIUS 0.058` ≈ 3 JND, `identity.materialDistance 0.025` ≈ 1.2 JND, `maximumSynthesizedOccupiedDistance 0.025` ≈ 1.2 JND. **Our thresholds are already at defensible perceptual multiples** — this is the main argument against a color-space migration.

### 1. Production systems

**Two architectures exist, neither is ours.** (a) *Extract a palette, assign roles by scoring* — Android Palette, node-vibrant, color-thief, Panic ColorArt/Apple lineage: preserves artwork colors, fails by returning null roles / illegible pairs / muddy averages. (b) *Extract one seed, synthesize everything* — Material You: always produces a complete contrast-guaranteed scheme, fails by discarding the artwork.

**MCU (Material Color Utilities).** `a < 255` → skip; `QuantizerCelebi(pixels, 128)`; `Score.score()`; keep **`ranked[0]` only**. Wu: `INDEX_BITS 5`, `SIDE_LENGTH 33`, `TOTAL_SIZE 35937`, prefix-sum moments, always split max-variance box, ties by lowest index (deterministic). WSMeans: weighted k-means in **CIELAB**, `MAX_ITERATIONS 10`, `MIN_MOVEMENT_DISTANCE 3.0`, triangle-inequality prune; seeds via `Math.random()` if Wu underfills, so strict determinism is not guaranteed. HCT = CAM16 hue/chroma + CIELAB L\* tone. HctSolver: ≤5 Newton iterations on J, else bisection over 12 cube edges + 255 critical planes; hue and tone held exact, returns the gamut-boundary color. Tone ladder (light/dark): surface/background 98/6, surfaceContainer `100/96/94/92/90` → `4/10/12/17/22`, onSurface 10/90, outline 50/60, primary 40/80. `ContrastCurve` at levels −1/0/0.5/1: onSurface `(4.5,7,11,21)`, onSurfaceVariant `(3,4.5,7,11)`, outline `(1.5,3,4.5,7)`. **T50 awkward zone**: tones 50–59 evacuated (`tonePrefersLightForeground = round(t) < 60`, `toneAllowsLightForeground = round(t) <= 49`). `DislikeAnalyzer`: `hue ∈ [90,111] && chroma > 16 && tone < 65` ⇒ fix to tone 70. `Score`: `TARGET_CHROMA 48`, `WEIGHT_PROPORTION 0.7`, `WEIGHT_CHROMA_ABOVE 0.3`, `WEIGHT_CHROMA_BELOW 0.1`, `CUTOFF_CHROMA 5.0`, `CUTOFF_EXCITED_PROPORTION 0.01`, hue dedup 90°→15°, fallback **Google Blue `0xff4285f4`**. Failure modes: greyscale art → Google Blue; TonalSpot pins chroma to **36** so a pastel and a neon sleeve of the same hue give identical themes; the alpha filter breaks on antialiased PNGs. AOSP `WallpaperColors`: downscale to 112×112, `clamp(area/16, 5, 128)` clusters, dark-text hint at mean luma > 0.7 with < 5% dark pixels.

**Android Palette / node-vibrant / color-thief.** Palette: 112×112 **nearest-neighbour** (deliberate — bilinear would invent colors), RGB555 histogram, 16 colors, **volume-ordered** heap, split at the **population median** of the longest dimension, swatch = population-weighted mean; filtered colors zeroed *before* boxing. `DEFAULT_FILTER`: L ≤ 0.05, L ≥ 0.95, red-I-line `hue ∈ [10,37] && sat ≤ 0.82`. Targets: `TARGET_DARK_LUMA 0.26 / MAX 0.45`, `MIN_LIGHT 0.55 / TARGET_LIGHT 0.74`, normal `0.3/0.5/0.7`, `TARGET_MUTED_SAT 0.3 / MAX 0.4`, `TARGET_VIBRANT_SAT 1.0 / MIN 0.35`; weights **sat 0.24 / luma 0.52 / pop 0.24**. `isExclusive()` defaults true → target order matters and a later target can come back null with no fallback. Text: contrast 3.0/4.5, white-for-both-or-black-for-both, alpha binary search (10 iters). node-vibrant: `colorCount 64`, `quality 5`, **MMCQ not ColorCut**, weights `3/6.5/0.5` (population ~5× weaker than Android); two real bugs — the `LightVibrant` fallback branch assigns `DarkVibrant`, and a muted fallback assigns the saturation constant `0.3` into *lightness*. color-thief/`quantize.js`: `sigbits 5`, `rshift 3`, `fractByPopulations 0.75`, phase-1 queue by **count** then phase-2 by **count×volume**; `doCut` at `partialsum > total/2` re-centred toward the longer side; **`avg()` uses bin centres `(i+0.5)*8`** — the direct cause of muddy color-thief output.

**Apple / iTunes 11.** Background = frequency mode of the **left-edge column** (skipping transparent columns), noise floor 1% of edge pixels, chromatic runner-up displaces black/white only if count **> 0.3×** the winner. `isBlackOrWhite`: all channels > 0.91 or < 0.09. `isDarkColor`: `0.2126r+0.7152g+0.0722b < 0.5`. `isDistinct`: any channel differs > **0.25** with a near-gray exception (`|r−g| < .03 && |r−b| < .03`). `minimumSaturation 0.15`. `isContrastingColor`: `(Llight+0.05)/(Ldark+0.05) > 1.6`. Text roles chosen greedily primary→secondary→detail with mutual distinctness. `UIImageColors` **drops the edge prior** (whole-image mode) — a documented degradation — and has a latent bug where a saturation-boosted color is looked up in the original count map, returning 0. **Real iTunes strips artwork borders first**; the open ports don't. Apple ships this as data (MusicKit `bgColor` + `textColor1..4`, server-computed at ingest); patents US 9,397,844 / 10,140,742 cover the border-cropping + background-validation step. Modern Apple Music's animated background is **not** palette extraction (four rotating blurred artwork copies + twist shader).

**Spotify et al. — honest negative result.** No first-party writeup. Internal GraphQL `fetchExtractedColors` returns `colorRaw/colorDark/colorLight {hex, isFallback}` — precomputed server-side, three contrast-clamped variants, explicit fallback flag. Best public reverse-engineering fits `S = 4.923·Chroma + 1.406·Darkness + 0.793·Dominance` and matches only **~31%** — treat any claimed Spotify formula as unverified. Nothing from Deezer, Plex, Jellyfin, Sonos, Tidal.

**The meta-finding:** both companies shipping this at scale compute it offline per asset and ship an explicit `isFallback` flag. That is an admission that no unsupervised rule works on every cover — and a hint that an honest low-confidence signal in our output may beat the last few points of accuracy.

**Corroboration for our constraints:** every production system downsamples to ~112×112 or a stride and emits synthesized bin averages. Their two signature artifacts — muddy averaged colors and gradients shattered into near-duplicates — are exactly what our full-res exact-pixel design avoids. Charter constraints 3 and 4 are validated, not obstacles. What's worth borrowing is the *shape* of their role logic: per-role targets with hard admissibility gates plus exclusivity, the closest published analogue to our role obligations — and its known pathology (later targets return null because earlier ones consumed the swatch) is structurally the `johns` failure under `maximumObligationsPerField: 4`.

### 2. Academic core

**Lin & Hanrahan, CHI 2013** — see TOP-8 #4. Additional detail: 40 candidate swatches/image from stratified-random-seeded k-means, **each centroid snapped to the nearest actual pixel** (same discipline we enforce); 160 MTurk workers × 5 swatches = 1600 themes, plus 11 art students; Turk ≈ artists; bottom 25% dropped. Metric `overlap(A,B,t)` and `dist` under min-cost bipartite matching; their hill-climbing "oracle" beats the average individual human. Test MAE 0.10 vs 0.22 baseline. **Corrections to common belief: there is no black/white-presence feature in this model** (that's Chang et al.); and **nameability is ≈ 0 and negative** (max |w| 0.0411), as are all color-name diversity terms, while plain mean-pairwise-CIELAB gets +1.5602. Their stated reason: highly nameable colors are less used in photographs and paintings.

**O'Donovan SIGGRAPH 2011** — 334-dim features, LASSO, MTurk MAE 0.179 vs 0.267. **No evidence hue templates predict compatibility** (themes near Matsuda/Itten templates rate slightly *lower*); entropy sweet spot 4.7–5.4 ≈ 2–3 hues; lightness dominates. **Do not adopt** — Lin & Hanrahan measured this aesthetics model as the *worst non-random method* for image representativeness. Aesthetics ≠ representativeness, and our charter says represent the artwork.

**Geometric.** Tan/Lien/Gingold TOG2016 (progressive hull, volume-minimizing edge contraction, deterministic, seconds); Tan et al. RGBXY 2018 (auto vertex count from an RMSE threshold, **6MP in 20ms, 48 lines of Python**); Wang CGF2019 (the rebuttal — a non-hull polyhedron whose vertices *are* representative). For artwork this is the real trade-off axis: hull = extreme/saturated/possibly imaginary; k-means = averaged/desaturated; Wang = constrained compromise.

**Chang et al. SIGGRAPH 2015** — a fully deterministic, very cheap palette extractor: 16³ RGB histogram, per-bin Lab means weighted by count (≤4096 points), greedy seeding by max weight with attenuation `(1 − exp(−d²/σ_a²))`, **σ_a = 80**; **black anchor** = a `(k+1)`th mean locked to black so dark pixels don't drag the others, discarded afterward. **60 ms** vs k-means 2 s. Worth reading for the seeding trick even though we don't cluster this way.

**Heer & Stone CHI 2012** — XKCD-survey-derived `p(w|c)`, color saliency from the entropy of `p(W|c)`, name distance via Hellinger or cosine. Works for visualization legends; **empirically worthless for image themes** — and also inconsistent with our own naming convention usage.

### 3. Skeptical flags — sounds good, don't do it

| Idea | Why it fails for us |
| --- | --- |
| MCU HCT tone synthesis | Synthesizes every role from one seed. Violates the source-connected constraint outright; empirically discards the artwork (chroma pinned to 36). |
| Any mean-emitting quantizer (Wu, WSMeans, MMCQ, ColorCut) | Emits synthesized colors; `(i+0.5)*8` bin-centre averaging is the textbook cause of muddy palettes. |
| Downsampling to 112×112 / striding | Universal in production; our own history documents that downscaling destroyed artwork-identity information. |
| **Saliency weighting** | Highest-magnitude feature in Lin & Hanrahan *and* the least transferable. Learned (Judd) saliency relies on face/horizon detectors that "usually fail on stylized images" — album art is stylized by definition. Achanta frequency-tuned is distance-from-image-mean, degenerating precisely when the salient object is large (the common album case). Hou spectral residual needs FFT at 64×64 — a downsample. If used at all: as a weight on evidence we already trust, never as a gate. |
| Color-name distinctness for diversity | LASSO weight ≈ 0, negative. |
| Harmony / hue templates and the O'Donovan compatibility score | No predictive evidence; worst non-random method for representativeness. Wrong objective for our charter. |
| **OKLab → CAM16-UCS / HCT migration** | Gains concentrate in blues; forces recalibration of every threshold in `policy.ts`, all currently at defensible 1–3 JND multiples. Certain cost, speculative benefit. **Skip.** |
| **SLIC as a family-formation alternative** | Deterministic and fast, but **SLIC happily cuts grain** — it does not solve texture-vs-structure. Its only value is node reduction, which our components already provide. **Skip.** |
| Mean-shift/EDISON, quick shift | Slow; mode-seeking on grainy dark fields produces exactly the spurious modes we're killing; skimage quick shift exposes an RNG. |
| Diffusion Curves, gradient meshes | Right mental model for `loups`, but a Poisson solve per image and no palette stops out the other end. Cite, don't implement. |
| Learned/ML palette models | Excluded by constraint — and unnecessary: a *linear* model already reaches human parity on this task. |

### 4. Two narrow contrast items (I9)
Both are **pathology detectors, not floors** — nothing here raises the APCA minimum.
- **T50 degeneracy.** MCU evacuates tones 50–59 because neither polarity reaches 4.5:1 there. Our analogue: a field in the lightness band where both polarities are equally weak is a field where `foregroundPolarityObservation()` is near-zero and near-noise. That is a principled detector for `greenday`-class field-polarity reversals — "this pair is reversible" is exactly a mid-band polarity-degeneracy test.
- **Helmholtz–Kohlrausch.** Saturated colors appear brighter than luminance predicts (strong in blues/reds, negligible in yellows); **APCA's public beta explicitly does not correct for it**. Since we deliberately operate at the bottom of the APCA range, the uncorrected bias is proportionally largest exactly where we live, and it is hue-dependent — chromatic and neutral accents are not judged on the same scale. Fairchild & Pirrotta's H-K lightness function is closed-form; apply only inside the APCA evidence term for chromatic colors, parameterized, defaulting off. *Caveat:* our contrast target is human-calibrated on our own outputs, so some H-K bias may already be baked into what reviewers approved.

### 5. I10 — border/frame prior audit (P2/P5; S)
Real iTunes strips borders before sampling; no open port does, and Panic calls framed sleeves a failure. Our `fieldScore` weights `borderCoverage` 0.22 and `quadrantCoverage` 0.13, and field-domain eligibility requires **≥2 owned corners** — so a framed or matted sleeve structurally favors the *frame* color as background. Cheap corpus audit (how many of our images are framed?) rather than an algorithm change, but if the count is non-trivial it's an unnamed failure class.

### 6. Also considered, lower priority
- **Binary Partition Tree (Salembier & Garrido) / scale-sets (Guigues)** — the formal version of a hierarchical region-adjacency graph with optimal-cut extraction. Worth reading before designing I1, since I1 is effectively one cut of such a hierarchy.
- **Granulometry / pattern spectrum** as a deterministic texture-scale descriptor via area attribute openings; mass below r≈3px as a "this field is textured" scalar.
- **MSER + distance-transform stroke variance** as a cheaper complement to full SWT.
- **Bradley–Terry over accumulated A/B preferences** — the warehouse stores per-item `preference` with true labels and an explicit `comparison: "identical"` flag (correctly excluded from preference mining). BT converts pairwise wins across all rounds into one latent quality score per label with CIs, so any round is comparable to any other without re-review. Standard in subjective IQA; borderline fittable now and improves free every round.
- **Adobe gradient patents US12450692 / US12340441** — a "rolling window" stop-merging rule; and **Chakraborty et al. CGF 2025** for a solid/linear/radial model-selection criterion.

### 7. Suggested ordering
1. **I12 + I11 + I13** (eval first — everything else is unmeasurable, and none of it risks the algorithm).
2. **I2** (texture gate) — smallest change with a named target (`meteora`).
3. **I3** (SWT) — biggest single unlock; `knuckles`, `krafty`, `slim`; orthogonal evidence.
4. **I6 → I5** (hue-direction prior, then log-det diversity) — P1, both drop into existing functions.
5. **I7** (gradient stops) — self-contained; its MDL penalty gives gradient neutrality a mechanism.
6. **I1** (FH predicate) — structurally right for P2/P5, highest regression risk; only after the metric can catch regressions.
7. **I4** (hull directions) — highest risk/payoff for P1; only after I5/I6 show the problem is *nomination*, not *ranking*.
8. **I8, I9, I10** — opportunistic.
