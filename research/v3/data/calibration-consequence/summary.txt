RULER CONSEQUENCE ANALYSIS — does the same-colour bar's remaining uncertainty change anything?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

THE QUESTION
  The same-colour bar is calibrated per region, but each region's threshold is a fitted point
  inside a 95% interval, and one region (light-saturated) is known not to be one population:
  split by hue it reads 0.01516 / 0.02074 / 0.03805. Both of those are recorded in
  constants.ts as open. This analysis prices them: if resolving the uncertainty the other way
  changes no decision, the bar can be frozen and a third bracketing round is not worth a
  reviewer's afternoon. If it changes decisions, the flips say which region to aim the round at.

WHAT WAS MEASURED
  Corpus: 554 real published palettes with reviewer verdicts (37 known-bad, 351 endorsements, 166 acceptable),
  492 distinct role signatures among them.
  Every pair the contract's distinctness invariant actually checks was enumerated by calling
  validateDistinctness() itself — the exemptions (sanctioned collapses, field-role-versus-stop)
  and the straddle rule are the contract's, not a copy. The invariant was then re-run once per
  bar variant and the verdicts compared pair by pair.

  Variants: point estimates (today's constants) | per-region CI low | per-region CI high |
  hue-split (light-saturated replaced by its three hue-third values, other regions unchanged).

  100000 pixel pairs from 200 sharded artworks, drawn at random and kept when their
  OKLab distance fell in [0.003, 0.05) — the band that strictly contains every region's window.
  Acceptance rate 8.95% of attempts; seed 2654435769.
  Inside the uncertainty window: 16715 of 100000 (16.71%).
  Below it (settled "same" whatever the calibration): 20750; above it (settled "distinct"): 62535.
  By the region that governs the pair:
    dark-neutral      34390 pairs,   8.04% inside the window
    dark-saturated    19092 pairs,  16.77% inside the window
    light-neutral     21110 pairs,  15.48% inside the window
    light-saturated   25408 pairs,  29.44% inside the window, 3906 judged differently by the hue split

WHAT THE NUMBERS SAY
  1. On the roles matrix — the decisions v3 will certainly make — the uncertainty is nearly inert: 0 flips at the low end,
     2 at the high end, 0 under the hue split, out of 3221 pairs. Today's constants call none of those 3221
     pairs the same colour: published role colours simply are not close to each other.
     Those 2 high-end flips are 1 distinct colour pair: the corpus holds one entry per reviewer verdict, so a
     palette endorsed twice is counted twice.
  2. Every flip anywhere is in a light or saturated region. 10 of the 12 high-end flips and all 3 hue-split flips
     involve a light-saturated colour; the rest are light-neutral pairs, and the low-end flips are light-neutral and
     dark-saturated stop pairs. Dark-neutral — the tightest and best-determined bar — never flips a single decision.
  3. The flips are directional. Widening the bar (ci-high, hue split) only ever *adds* violations (15 to-same,
     0 to-distinct); narrowing it only ever removes them (7 to-distinct, 0 to-same). There is no churn, only a monotone dial.
  4. 12 of the 20 validity changes (counted per matrix; the two matrices overlap) would newly
     condemn a palette the reviewer endorsed or accepted.
     PHASE_0_DECISIONS §4 says an invariant that blocks an endorsed palette is demoted — so the upper end of the
     interval, and the loosest hue third, are the ends the corpus actively argues against. The lower end costs nothing
     but silence: its flips only release pairs, and a released pair is never seen again.
  5. The uncertainty is common in the *world* and rare in the *decisions*: 16.71% of real artwork near-pairs
     land inside a window (29.44% of light-saturated ones), against 0.34% of published pairs at worst. Published palette
     colours are chosen to be far apart, so they sit almost entirely outside the range the measurement is unsure about.

RECOMMENDATION — FREEZE the bar at its point estimates. No round 3 for the same-colour bar.

  Freezing costs at most 2 palette verdicts out of 554 on the matrix that matters, and every one of those
  disagreements is the *loose* end of the interval wanting to condemn a palette the reviewer endorsed. A round 3
  that halved the intervals again would, on this evidence, change between 0 and 2 role-level verdicts. That is not
  a reviewer's afternoon well spent, and the reviewer's own repeat consistency is 63% — a third
  round cannot buy precision the reviewer does not have.

  Three things to keep, not because they change decisions today but because they are cheap:
  - **If any round 3 is ever run, aim it at light-saturated, and split it by hue.** Every flip that touches the
    roles matrix is light-saturated, its interval is the widest (0.01658–0.03170, 1.9×), and the hue split inside it
    is the one finding with a real effect behind it (2.5× spread, third 2 separated). Nothing would be learned by
    re-measuring dark-neutral: it is the tightest bar and it moves nothing.
  - **Do not adopt the hue split as it stands.** It flips only toward *more* violations, all of them on endorsed or
    accepted palettes (3 pairs, all from third 2's 0.03805 — the value that is the middle of a separation gap, not
    a fitted crossing). Encoding it would make the contract stricter exactly where it is least sure.
  - **The anisotropy finding is untouched by this analysis.** It is not a width in the bar, it is a missing dimension,
    and no variant here can price it. If a round 3 is ever justified, that is the better question to spend it on.
