# Prior-art check: the six Phase-1 mechanisms against everything this repo has already tried

**Question (reviewer, verbatim):** "does one of those 6 mechanisms match what has already been tried in this repo?"

**Method.** Six parallel Opus subagents, each with a narrow read-only surface: (1) root pipeline code (`extractColors.ts`, `foregroundDetection.ts`, `gradientDetection.ts`, `kmeans/`, `visual.ts`); (2) v2 early-mid era (main pipeline `research/src/album-artwork-palette-v2.ts`, plans, phase 3/4 postmortems, field guide); (3) v2 late era (0.7.x–0.9, `autonomousPalette.ts`, chromatic-role); (4) plans and field-state documents (`next-palette`, `native-complete-palette`, field-state calibration, scale-space audit); (5) git archaeology (636 commits, 60+ unmerged `worktree-agent-*` branches, 254 commits unreachable from HEAD); (6) mechanism fingerprints of the fourteen proposals from `DIVERGENCE_MAP.md` / `DIVERGENCE_MAP_2.md`. Verdicts below are reconciled across those six reports; every claim carries its file or commit evidence.

**One structural fact first** (git sweep, 10,483 paths ever / 4,400 now): there is **no buried alternative architecture**. All 636 commits are one lineage — quantise pixels to colour families → generate candidate palettes → score on a multi-axis objective → elect a winner. Only four runtime modules were ever deleted. What the current tree hides is 31 experiment lines and ~168 classified kills on unmerged branches (`research/v2-3-experiments/graveyard-audit/REPORT.md`, commit `2aa445a`: 67% evidence-backed kills).

## P1 — one global objective priced as description length / model order

**Verdict: half tried.** The two halves have opposite histories.

*The whole-object half was tried, repeatedly, and survives.* The unit of comparison in v2 is the complete treatment — four roles, gradient state, collapse flags created and compared as one object (`research/src/album-artwork-palette-v2.ts:3671 createTreatment`, `:4089 selectCompletePaletteTreatmentDomain`, ≤1,500 enumerated). `research/src/joint-palette.ts:393` scores bg/fg/surface/accent/gradient together; `research/src/next-palette.ts:413 solveNextPalette` enumerates complete `(background, foreground, surface, accent, gradientState)` tuples under hard constraints ("Gradient is therefore selected jointly with its endpoints rather than patched onto a selected pair" — `NEXT_PALETTE_ARCHITECTURE.md:119`). This was the successor to root-era greedy sequential selection, whose trap failure is recorded verbatim in `foregroundDetection.ts:2113-2118` (birdsofprey).

*The pricing half was never built.* `MDL`, `description length`, `model order` have **zero occurrences in the diff history before 2026-08-04** — every hit is a v3 document. `ALBUM_ARTWORK_UI_PALETTE_FIELD_GUIDE.md:92` states it outright: "A principled alternative we never built: price the gradient claim symmetrically inside the fit itself — see the MDL idea in §6." The only model-order-by-scalar machinery ever shipped prices K, the cluster count (`kmeans/gapStatistic.ts`), not the palette. Gradient-vs-flat and collapse-vs-distinct were always decided by separate gates, never by one constant repaid by explanatory gain.

*What was tried and failed is neither half.* A single hand-weighted scalar over the whole object existed (`rankingScore`, v2 `:3616`) and was deliberately demoted: "Historical weighted scores remain diagnostics only" (`ALBUM_ARTWORK_UI_PALETTE_PROTOCOL_V2.md`) — and one targeted review recorded evidence *for* it without reinstatement (`analyze-album-artwork-palette-v2-0.3.0-targeted-review.ts:82`). Track V's attempt to fit the 11 objective axes to reviewer preferences degraded held-out agreement 66.7%→51.9% (`worktree-agent-a0658e96a59e92be4`, `1de494d`). `adversarial-objective/REVIEW.md` (`9f1b172`) diagnosed the shipped objective as layers "encoding different theories of 'better' and fighting each other." Per the fingerprint distinctions, a *hand-weighted multi-term scalar with several exchange rates is P6's shape, not P1's* — so these failures do not count against P1's single-exchange-rate coding cost, but they do mean the repo has never once seen a scalar objective survive.

**Materially different?** Yes. A/A′ differ from every prior attempt in the three places that matter: the currency (bits, one exchange rate, contract schema as the model prior), the feasible set (contract shrinks it, never a term), and the search (certified branch-and-bound with an optimality gap, able to score palettes it did not produce). None of that has an ancestor here. The nearest true precedent — joint enumeration of complete tuples — is the part of P1 the prior work validated, not the part it refuted.

**Erratum carried from the fingerprint audit:** `arm-e-r3` does not satisfy P1 as grouped. Its description length prices the ground/field half only; foreground and accent come from a five-level lexicographic ordering no currency evaluates — the same exclusion `DIVERGENCE_MAP_2.md` §3.2 applied to C′. Mechanically it sits beside P5. This matters for prior-art exposure: e-r3's lexicographic F/A pool revives the v2 comparator shape at the exact site the phase-3 postmortem blamed ("Top-one ranking is the main failure" — 8 of 10 failures first failed in ranking, `ALBUM_ARTWORK_UI_PALETTE_PHASE_3_FINAL_SHOWCASE_POSTMORTEM.md`), and a v2-3 attempt to retire that comparator found nothing better (graveyard T3 leximin: "0 fixes anywhere on 171 images").

## P2 — hierarchical region decomposition

**Verdict: half tried — the flat half is the incumbent; the tree never existed.**

Roles read off materialised regions is this repo's oldest working mechanism: background = largest 4-connected component of the nearest-centroid label map (`extractColors.ts:208-275`); border-flood background ranking (`foregroundDetection.ts rankBackgroundCandidates`); an explicit region-adjacency graph in `tripletMethod` (`foregroundDetection.ts:1806-1885`); SLIC superpixels + flat region graph in the 0.19 baseline (`research/src/regions.ts:109`); v2's family components and one-λ quasi-flat field domains (`buildBackgroundFieldDomains:1975` — growth gated by `meanContrast ≤ familyAnchorRadius`, a quasi-flat zone at exactly one threshold).

The hierarchy itself was proposed twice and built zero times. `ALBUM_ARTWORK_UI_PALETTE_PLAN.md` §"Region hierarchy" asks for "membership in larger parent regions"; `grep -c parent` over the 5,838-line implementation returns 0. Phase-3 postmortem corrective item 9 is literally "Explore A Hierarchical Region-Adjacency Graph" — never run. The complete region graph that *was* built once was discarded after transition discovery (`album-artwork-palette-v2-phase-3-field-transition.ts:220-357`). Component tree / max-tree / tree of shapes / quasi-flat hierarchy / watershed / α-tree: every diff-level occurrence in 636 commits is dated 2026-08-04 (the proposals themselves).

**Materially different?** Yes for the load-bearing part — nesting, attribute filters (MSER stability, isoperimetric ratio, counters), ordinal weightless rules, and gradient-as-tree-shape have no ancestor. Two cautions. First, the flat substrate the proposals share ("the field is what reaches the frame", background = frame-touching zone) is precisely the prior mechanism — which mostly *worked*; the incumbent's recorded kills (dither instability, id relabel) were in colour-space point estimates, which F′ names as its target. Second, an erratum the git agent caught: the Phase-1 toolbox gap scan (`7d6f570`) claims superpixels are "already built (`research/src/regions.ts`)" — `regions.ts` is the flat region graph; the claim as phrased is wrong and should not be relied on.

## P3 — per-pixel scalar fields and ranks

**Verdict: not tried.** The strongest genuine novelty among the six.

Per-pixel scalar fields were built three times: Itti–Koch centre-surround saliency (2024, `saliency/saliency.worker.ts`, still load-bearing in production via the saliency-delta signal); the Stroke Width Transform field (Track T, `research/v2-3/src/internal/stroke-width.ts` @ `a67e82d`, measured and never integrated); and the Q0.24 exact box low-pass fields of the scale-space audit (`research/src/native-scale-space-evidence.ts`, whose plan *forbids* role output: "No selector, solver, role-policy… may be imported, invoked, instantiated, or serialized"). In every case the field feeds a clustering/candidate pipeline as a weight or a diagnostic. The defining P3 move — the published colour *is* the pixel at a designated rank, with no candidate object of cardinality ≠ N anywhere — has no ancestor: v2 representative strategies are `dense-exact | nearest-prototype | density-synthesized` (one of which synthesises a colour); "Candidate availability precedes ranking" is doctrine (`PLAN_V2:817`); every historical use of rank/percentile ranks candidate palettes, not pixels.

**Materially different?** Entirely. Two flags for Phase 2: (a) both D arms establish that reachability-against-endorsed is vacuous for P3 (100% by construction), so it cannot be scored on that axis; (b) D′'s stated contrast — "v2-3-shaped systems compute an ideal field colour and then find the nearest real pixel" — is confirmed by the audit: that snap sub-mechanism is real prior art (`endpointBandRepresentatives`, v2 `:2289-2363`), and arm-f / arm-f-r3 retain it (continuous target, project at the end). P3 and P1 are the only groups that refuse it.

## P4 — portfolio of rival extractors under a selector

**Verdict: partially tried — and the highest-risk match of the six.** The pattern across three eras is unambiguous: **portfolios are cheap and get built every time; the selector is where this repo dies.**

- Root era: seven complete rival palettes per image (`extractColors.ts:568-617` — current, hybrid, vibrant, multiPass, unified, paired, triplet, each with its own gradient decision) and four rival foreground methods (`detectForegroundAllMethods`). **No selector** — production hardcodes `foregroundMethods.original.color` (`:297`), and election happens in `visual.ts`'s comparison grid, by a human. Three more methods were written and never wired in. A small automated combiner *did* exist — two gradient detectors plus an agreement score — and was deleted; `example-gradient-detection.ts` still imports its ghost API.
- 0.19 baseline: three rival outputs (`spatial | expressive | quantized`, `src/types.ts`), consumer hardcodes `spatial`.
- `autonomousPalette.ts:356-441`: three rival readings (textLed / structureLed / contrastLed) elected by `confidence = score × reliability` — the only runtime score-based election ever shipped, but foreground-only, over shared evidence, with hand-constant reliabilities (contrastLed hardcoded 0.65).
- v2-2 parallel arms + `recovery-selector-v2/v4` (deleted at `4073235`, byte-equivalent survivors in `research/src/`): a portfolio over candidate *treatments inside one extractor*, elected by protocol gate and human review, not per-image machinery.
- Field-state electors: the two attempts to *earn* selection authority from human preference both failed their gates — `field-state-calibration-analysis.json` (`decision.status: "withhold"`) and `factorized-field-state-analysis.json` (pair accuracy 0.5758 vs 0.60 gate; "Factorized field authority is withheld"). Track V (above) is the same failure one layer up.

The 0.7.6 discovery arms are **not** P4 — offline one-factor ablations elected once by protocol ("Stop after the first arm passes", `PROTOCOL_V2_0_7_6.md:79`), no runtime election. And per the fingerprint, a dispatch classifier would not be P4 either; nothing of that shape existed anyway.

**Materially different?** Only in the selector — which is exactly the right place, and exactly the unproven one. C/C′'s currency is member-independent (members "emit no score, no confidence and no palette"): within-image coverage mass (C), or description length in bits with a block-bootstrap margin and an outputs-agree/immateriality check (C′). No prior selector had any of those properties — every prior one was a hand-weighted confidence, a fitted preference model that failed its gate, or a human. The prior art therefore does not refute P4, but it concentrates all risk in one claim: that a non-fitted, member-independent currency succeeds where four fitted/hand-tuned selectors failed. Phase 2 should treat the selector, not the members, as the thing under test.

## P5 — robust parametric field fit plus residual marks

**Verdict: partially tried — the fit without the robustness and without the marks; the missing parts target the recorded failure.**

The parametric fit is real prior art: `fitGradients` (v2 `:2260-2382`) regresses OKLab per channel on a normalised spatial parameter t over 12×12 population-weighted cell means, for 6 (later 13) fixed geometries, computing slope/intercept/weighted-RMS residual/monotonicity. Three structural inversions relative to P5, each load-bearing:

1. **Segment-first, fit-second.** The fit only ever sees pixels already flood-filled into an eligible field domain (`buildBackgroundFieldDomains:1897`); outliers are excluded by pre-segmentation. P5 inverts this: the redescending loss *is* the segmentation ("no mask, no connected-component pass, no size threshold, no morphology" — arm-f).
2. **No robust loss, ever.** IRLS / Tukey / biweight / Huber / M-estimator / RANSAC: zero hits in 636 commits before 2026-08-04. Plain (weighted) least squares throughout; even root-era near-misses (elbow-curve OLS with IQR trimming) are off-image.
3. **Residual as veto, never as marks.** `residual` feeds a rejection reason ("fit residual exceeds 0.9 of transition span", `:2562`) and a 0.25-weight score term; foreground and accent come from independent family lanes. "The overlay is what the fit rejects" has no ancestor — though `PLAN_V2` lists "residual error and unexplained structure" among evidence to retain, unbuilt.

The prior gradient sub-project's terminal limitation is stated in `research/GRADIENT_DETECTION_CHECKPOINT.md`: "The eligibility and topology branches only test whether the already selected directed background/surface pair should remain a gradient. They do not select another pair or repair role assignment." P5 is the proposal that dissolves that ordering — field first, roles from the field and its rejections.

**Materially different?** Yes, and in the diagnosed direction. Two residual exposures: arm-f-r3 *forces* the gradient boolean (true iff projected ends differ under the bar) where prior history shows narrow gradient thresholds never converged ("The sequence did not converge… narrow threshold and exception fitting… is closed" — GRADIENT_DETECTION_CHECKPOINT) — forcing is a different mechanism from thresholding, but it inherits the same review pressure; and the fitted-target→nearest-pixel projection both F arms retain is the v2 endpoint-snap sub-mechanism D′ indicts by name (see P3). Also note the fingerprint erratum: `arm-e-r3` is mechanically this group's third member.

## P6 — one continuous joint figure–ground measure

**Verdict: partially tried — new substrate, relapsed objective shape.** This is the one place a v3 proposal re-enters a documented failure mode.

*What is genuinely unprecedented.* No prior system ever had surround at more than one scale (root saliency: fixed radius-3 window, `saliency.worker.ts:19`; v2: one-pixel `localContrast` plus adjacency edge counts). No prior system deferred quantisation: root packs OKLab to 8-bit triples as its first operation; v2's first step is the 0.04 grid (`quantizedKey:1058`), and the field guide records its cost ("±20% moves ~97% of palettes"; the grid origin sits on the neutral axis). E′'s blur-ladder surround, habitual ground B(v) (answerable before the background is known), and quantise-exactly-once-at-the-final-argmin have no ancestor. The nearest survivor is Track D's diffuse multiscale field composite (merged at `c496827`) — evidence, not a measure. "Figure-ground" as a term appears nowhere pre-v3.

*What was tried, repeatedly, and failed — and matches E′'s objective shape.* Per the fingerprint audit's own boundary ruling, "scored whole (bg, surface, fg, accent) tuples with a weighted multi-term objective containing pairwise terms and several exchange rates" **is P6's shape, not P1's** (it is why E′ is excluded from P1: "seven named term families with exchange rates that E′ itself counts as free parameters 3, 4, 6 and 7"). That shape is this repo's most-relapsed failure:

- Root: `pairedMethod`/`tripletMethod` joint tuple enumeration under ad-hoc score products (`foregroundDetection.ts:2136-2218, :1794-2103`) — the products' constants were per-fixture repairs ("fixes birdsofprey", "fixes knuckles").
- v2: the single weighted scalar demoted to diagnostics; non-compensatory blocks imposed by policy ("must not… permit one stage to compensate for a resolved loss at another", `0_7_1_QUALITY_GUARD_POSTMORTEM.md:305`).
- v2-3: Track V weight-fitting refuted (66.7%→51.9%); `adversarial-objective` diagnosis of layered theories fighting; contextual-accent's relational scoring "VALLEY on the arm's own concept" (`f637e49`).

E′'s defence is explicit and falsifiable: the prior failures happened over near-tied *quantised* bins, and "the default is what produced a system a ±1 LSB dither moved 114 times out of 114"; if palettes still move at a comparable rate, "the instability was never in the quantisation stage and my diagnosis was mistaken." That is a real difference — every prior multi-term objective ranged over quantised candidates — but the exchange-rate problem is orthogonal to quantisation, and nothing in the prior record suggests continuity cures it. **Named loudly: E′'s energy shape is the single closest approach any v3 proposal makes to reinventing a known failure.** The substrate novelty may still carry it; the term weights are where Phase 2 scrutiny belongs.

## Coverage holes — tried here, reached by no v3 proposal

1. **Fitting decision models to human preference labels.** Bradley–Terry field-state rankers (twice built, twice withheld), the frozen logistic gradient classifier (`gradient-field-topology-model.ts` — one of its three coefficients fit to exactly 0), Track V weight learning (refuted), and the proposed-only Davidson veto model + frozen visual reranker (`NATIVE_COMPLETE_PALETTE_EXECUTION_PLAN.md` Phases 7-8). No proposal fits anything to human labels. Given the failure record this looks like a deliberate hole, but the *evidence protocol* half (single-factor forced-choice supervision, closed for interpretability reasons: "judgments cannot be interpreted independently when the fixed foreground… is unacceptable", `COMPLETE_PALETTE_ONLY_PLAN.md:5-9`) is knowledge no proposal inherits.
2. **Incumbent-anchored replacement.** Emit the previous canonical palette unless a challenger strictly dominates (`joint-palette.ts:650`, native-complete-palette's Pareto preservation — which review judged "a much slower wrapper around 0.19", `PALETTE_EXTRACTION_QUALITY_RESET_HANDOFF.md:41-57`). No proposal has output-stability-by-anchoring; v3 pursues stability through determinism instead. Worth one line in Phase 2: the anchor was also the mechanism that froze improvement.
3. **Shape evidence as a first-class signal.** Stroke Width Transform (Track T) and the two root-era text-region detectors (`edgeDetection.ts`, `textRegions.ts`). P2's glyph attributes (thinness, counters, collinearity) partially reach this; nothing else does; SWT specifically was measured and never falsified as evidence (its *veto* use was).
4. **The saliency-delta distributional lift** — production's single load-bearing foreground signal (saliency-mass share minus area share, `extractColors.ts:172-205`). No proposal computes an over-representation-relative-to-area statistic; the nearest relative is E′'s displacement measure.
5. **Colour synthesis under a contrast floor.** `increaseContrast` — dead at birth in 2024 ("not super pretty"), zero live call sites, yet re-live as a contract question (`83c644c`: "the one non-source colour escape"). All six mechanisms publish source colours; the contract's escape hatch has no mechanism behind it in any proposal.
6. **Semantic segmentation (VLM/SAM) route** — in-tree, downgraded to "unwanted" at runtime (`ba9e000`); no proposal uses it. Recording as a hole for completeness, not as a recommendation.
7. **Adaptive perceptual quantiser grain** (`family-grain.ts`, shipped OFF) — representation-level work no proposal needs (P3/P6 have no quantiser to re-grain; P6's 48³ lattice is quadrature with Lipschitz reads, a different object).
8. **Bounded candidate supplementation by hue anchor** — notable as the late era's *only promotion-eligible success* (`chromatic-role-extract.ts:56`; reserve-validation 0.8.0: 4 candidate-preferred / 0 baseline-preferred). Its lesson — recall failures are fixed by widening availability, not by re-ranking — is structural in P1/P3/P6 (no candidate wall to widen) but must be re-earned by P2/P4/P5, whose node sets and member rosters are new candidacy walls.

## Capability test — the middle orchestration tier, as data

**Did subagent spawning work from inside a subagent?** Yes, cleanly. I had the Agent tool, launched **six Opus subagents in a single message**, and all six ran concurrently in the background (wall times 230s–639s, fully overlapped; total elapsed ≈ 11 minutes for ~600 files/commits of reading). Zero failures, zero retries, zero collisions — all six were read-only by brief, so path ownership was trivial. Completion arrived as asynchronous notifications, one per agent, which re-invoke the orchestrator; that interleaving was orderly at n=6.

**What the tier costs.** Subagents spent ≈ 850k tokens between them (112k–171k each). Of my own context, roughly: 10% recon + brief-writing, 55% ingesting the six reports (~2–6k tokens each — the briefs' "terse, evidence or silence" clause is what kept this affordable), 35% reconciliation + this synthesis. The decomposition was **clearly worth it at this task size**: the corpus (636 commits, 254 unreachable, ~90 research modules, 30+ postmortems) exceeds any single context, and the redundancy paid twice — agents disagreed on P1 (PARTIAL vs ABSENT) because each saw a different half of the mechanism, and the disagreement itself produced the correct verdict ("half tried, and the halves have opposite histories"). A single reader would likely have collapsed that.

**Where the structure would strain at six-prototypes scale.**
1. **Sequencing I got away with ignoring.** I ran the proposal-fingerprint agent in parallel with the prior-art agents, so the audit agents graded against my brief's group definitions, which the fingerprint agent then partially corrected (e-r3 misfiled; P1/P4 currency overlap). I reconciled after the fact; at scale, fingerprints/specs should land *before* audit briefs — a serial dependency that eats the parallelism win on the critical path.
2. **Report-size discipline is the whole game.** Six reports fit; six prototypes × many agents each will not, unless every tier enforces hard caps and verdict vocabularies (TRIED/PARTIAL/ABSENT with mandatory evidence) in the brief. Free-prose reports would have overflowed me at ~2× this scale.
3. **Notification fan-in.** One orchestrator × 6 agents was orderly. Six mid-tier orchestrators each fanning out means the global orchestrator's re-invocations multiply; mid-tiers must absorb their agents' completions and report once, not stream.
4. **Write-path collisions were avoided only because subagents were read-only.** In Phase 2 the sub-agents will write code; per-agent path ownership must be in every brief, not assumed.
5. **What I could not do:** verify subagent claims cheaply. I spot-checked nothing — the cross-agent redundancy (three agents independently grepping for IRLS/MDL, two dating all tree-vocabulary to 2026-08-04) substituted for verification. Briefs should deliberately overlap one probe per pair of agents; accidental overlap is what saved me here.

**Blunt bottom line:** for this task the tier was worth it — the corpus is unreadable by one agent, and the disagreements were informative rather than costly. The tier is *not* worth it for tasks whose corpus fits one context; the overhead (briefs, reconciliation, erratum-chasing across agents) is ~30% of the orchestrator's budget regardless of task size.
